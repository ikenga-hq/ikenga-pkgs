Add per-user policies: an optional `userPolicies` block per bot, keyed by Mattermost user id, lets someone who is not in `allowedUsers` use the bot in the policy's own `channels` with their own `cwd`, `systemPrompt` and an explicit `allowedTools` list. The bridge sends the limits to the daemon as `opts.toolPolicy` on `chi_run` (`tools`, `allowedTools`, `disallowedTools`, `mode: "dontAsk"`, `strictMcp`, `settingSources`), and fails closed: it starts or resumes no policy run unless the daemon's `/api/health` advertises the `chi.toolPolicy` capability (checked live every time, at startup and on reconnect; a refusal is said in the thread and recorded in the audit log). The only enforcer is the daemon (`enforcedBy: "daemon"`); there is no settings-file mode. Validated at load: unknown tool names; an exact-allowlist `Bash` rule (`Bash(<bash|sh|node|python3> <relative script>[:*])`, script not group/world-writable and not owned by the bot's uid when it exists); `Read`/`Grep`/`Glob`/`LS` rules scoped relative inside the cwd; a policy `cwd` that is, contains or sits inside `chi.cwd` or any new `privateRoots` entry (after `~`, normalisation and symlink resolution); username entries in `allowedUsers` on a bot with policies, or a policy id that is one; a missing `enforcedBy`; a mode above `maxMode`. Policy channels are resolved once to channel ids and matched by id only. `disallowedTools` defaults to every known tool not in `tools`. Members of `allowedUsers` are unchanged, a user on no list is refused as before, and a thread is owned by the session that started it. New operator commands `--list-policies` and `--print-policy <bot>/<user id>` (diagnostic). `maxMode` now also accepts `default`. Configs without `userPolicies` behave exactly as before.
